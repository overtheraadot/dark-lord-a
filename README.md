A make.fun entry for "The Tired Dark Lord: A 100-Word Villain Monologue" — one monologue of exactly 100 words, spoken by a fantasy villain ground down by middle-management: minion HR paperwork, dungeon maintenance tickets, performance reviews for henchmen.

## The entry

> Heroes. Sit. Not the throne— the stool. The throne broke in the third age, the work order's still under the goblin union's grievance forms.
>
> You want the sword? Third rack, armoury, behind the cursed helms nobody signed out. Form nine-B. Triplicate. The last champion forgot, and now I own his soul and his filing cabinet.
>
> I had a plan once. Storm the kingdoms. Raise the dead. Now I raise purchase orders. The dead, at least, never asked for a performance review.
>
> Strike, hero— please, make it quick. The minions' rota needs approving by dawn, and only I can read it.

570 characters, 100 words. The tiredness is felt, never announced — no line says he is tired. He is still a villain: he owns the last champion's soul, and he had a plan once. The sting is in the last line: the all-powerful Dark Lord is the only one in the dungeon who can read the rota.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

`main.py` (Python stdlib only) validates the entry against the gate in RULES.txt — UTF-8 text, 1 to 600 characters, not only white space — and the brief — exactly 100 words, with the weariness felt rather than announced — then writes it to `/output/entry.txt`.
